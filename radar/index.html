      🛠️ Build your own Arduino radar

      A servo motor swings an ultrasonic sensor back and forth like a radar dish. The sensor sends out a "ping" of sound (too high to hear) and times the echo to work out how far away things are.

      3. Upload the code
      1. Install the free Arduino IDE on a computer.
      2. Make a new sketch, delete what's in it, and paste in the code below (or download it).
      3. Plug in the Arduino. Pick Tools → Board → Arduino Uno and the right Port.
      4. Press Upload (the → arrow).
      5. Close the IDE's Serial Monitor if it's open (only one program can use the Arduino at a time).
      6. Open this page in Chrome or Edge, pick 🔌 My Arduino at the top, and press Connect.

      4. Make it portable (handheld, military style)

      Turn it into a handheld unit you can carry around, with its own screen and battery.

      1. Push the Proto Screw Shield onto the Uno. Push the bare end of each jumper wire into a screw terminal and tighten it. Do the wiring before the screen goes on, because the screen partly covers the screws.
      2. Wire the screw terminals: sensor (Trig → 11, Echo → 12), servo (signal → 10), buzzer (+ → A5) and button (pin 13 and GND), with 5V and GND for the sensor and servo. Then push the screen onto the screw shield's sockets.
      3. Upload the Handheld (2.8" screen) code from step 3.
      4. Mount the servo at the front of the box, on top or through a slot, so the sensor can swing 150° and look forward.
      5. Plug in the power bank and you have a portable radar. Press the button to switch range: 50 cm → 1 m → 2 m → 4 m.

      On the screen: a yellow square is a tracked object with its number (T01…), a red diamond is a THREAT (inside 50 cm, or racing toward you), and the top lines show the bearing, range, speed and whether it's CLOSING, OPENING, CROSSING or HOLDING.

      Upgrade for a real radar: swap the ultrasonic sensor for a 24 GHz mmWave radar module such as the HLK-LD2450 (~$15). It's a true radio radar (no servo needed): it tracks up to 3 moving targets at once up to about 6 m and gives their position and speed. It's more advanced to code, so get the ultrasonic version working first.
      5. What can it actually detect?

      The ultrasonic sensor sees things from 2 cm to about 4 m away, in a narrow cone (about 15° wide) straight in front of it. It's best with things that are bigger than your hand and have flat sides. So it can spot:

      • ✅ A paper plane or a ball flying past close by, your hand, a person walking by
      • ✅ A toy drone hovering near it (the spinning props make it a bit jumpy)
      • ❌ Planes, birds or anything far up in the sky: sound doesn't go that far and bounce back
      • ❌ Soft or fluffy things (curtains, a cat's fur) soak up the sound

      Want to detect real aircraft? Real radar uses radio waves, not sound. The easiest way at home is to listen for planes instead: every airliner broadcasts its position on 1090 MHz.

      • 📻 Your own plane receiver: an RTL-SDR USB radio stick with a 1090 MHz antenna (~$30–40) plugged into a computer or Raspberry Pi, running the free dump1090 or readsb program. It picks up planes 100+ miles away. That's exactly where the ✈️ Real planes data comes from: thousands of people with these.
      • 🌊 Motion sensors: an RCWL-0516 or HB100 microwave sensor (~$1–5) uses real radio "radar" to sense movement through plastic and thin walls. It tells you something moved, not how far.
      • 🚁 Drones: spotting drones that don't broadcast is hard (even for professionals), so it's out of reach for an Arduino.
