# idk
My personal website, built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript and hosted on GitHub Pages.

Live at: https://the1miknoob.github.io/idk/

## What's inside
- **Games** (`games/`): Block Pop, Merge Drop, Word Hunt, Night Swarm, Sky Hop, Snake, 2048, Reaction Lab and Chess
- **Music Player** (`music-player/`): play your own songs with skins, visualizers and stickers
- **Personal Dashboard** (`dashboard/`): clock, weather, to-dos, notes, focus timer and more
- **World Watch** (`world-watch/`): a live world map with news, conflict zones, war news, earthquakes, storms, the space station, live news TV, markets and famous battles
- **Armor Pen Sim** (`armor-sim/`): a particle physics sandbox where shells, bullets, darts, rockets and missiles smash into armor you build yourself (steel, ceramics, uranium, glass, reactive tiles, sandbags, water, ice, oak…) and plates bend, crack, heat up and spall. Solve each shot with particles (full physics) or instantly with formulas (Alekseevskii–Tate erosion, Rosenberg ricochet, Recht–Ipson exit speed, Grady fragments), plate by plate. Try strange darts (plutonium, gold, osmium, diamond), old-time guns (musket balls, cannonballs) and air-to-air missiles (AIM-9 Sidewinder, AMRAAM, Sparrow, Phoenix, R-73, R-77) on a fighter jet's skin or a tank. After a rocket, missile or explosive shell goes off, the rest of it (a motor tube, tail fins, a heavy base) still slams into the armor. Shoot at 28 whole vehicles made of particles (Tiger I and II, Maus, KV-1, T-34-85, Sherman, Churchill, the Bob Semple tank, T-72, Leopard 1 and 2, M1 Abrams, Merkava, T-90, T-14 Armata…): see what happens to the crew, ammo and engine inside, blow the turret off, keep shooting the wreck, fire up to 8 shots at once (side by side or one after another), take on 16 challenges, or build your own vehicle and share it with a link. Make your own projectiles too (solid shots, darts, shaped charges, squash heads or explosive shells, from any material) and share them with a link. Also has a quick calculator, a target range with made-up tanks, and a Learn tab
- **Study Buddy** (`study/`): type any topic and it reads up on it on Wikipedia, then makes notes (summary, key facts, timeline, key words, an outline), flashcards, a quiz (multiple choice, true or false, type the answer), a matching game and a typing drill. Math topics get endless practice problems with worked steps (arithmetic to quadratics, Pythagoras, area, statistics, primes). Simple English option, read aloud, and your sets and progress are saved for offline studying
- **Browser** (`browser/`): a web browser inside the site. Every page goes through a proxy: [Scramjet](https://github.com/MercuryWorkshop/scramjet) (the default, made for big modern sites like YouTube) or [Ultraviolet](https://github.com/titaniumnetwork-dev/Ultraviolet) runs in the page and sends traffic through an encrypted tunnel ([epoxy](https://github.com/MercuryWorkshop/epoxy-tls), via [bare-mux](https://github.com/MercuryWorkshop/bare-mux)) to a free [Wisp](https://github.com/MercuryWorkshop/wisp-protocol) proxy server, so websites see the proxy instead of you and https pages stay encrypted the whole way. Has tabs, back and forward, a search bar (DuckDuckGo, Google, Bing, Brave or Wikipedia), bookmarks and a bookmarks bar, history, site icons, shortcuts on the new tab page, zoom and keyboard shortcuts. Customize it from the new tab page: light or dark theme, any accent color, classic or rounded tabs, compact mode, font, the bookmarks bar, the home button, and the new tab page's background (colors or your own picture), clock, shortcuts and lists. The free public proxy servers come and go, so Automatic tests a list of them (each has to really load a small page), uses one that works, and switches to another by itself if pages stop loading. If a site won't load (free servers often block big sites like YouTube), it quietly tries that site through the other servers and switches to one that can open it. Videos play through the proxy (seeking and streaming players included). On YouTube, whose full page often refuses to play videos for free proxy servers ("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"), the Browser watches the player, and if a video won't play it switches to YouTube's simple embedded player at the same moment; a bar under the toolbar also offers another server, the other proxy engine, or an Invidious site (Settings → YouTube videos: Automatic, Simple player or Full YouTube page). You can also pick a server or use your own
- **Nuke Sim** (`nuke-sim/`): a nuclear war simulation on a satellite world map, styled like the well-known nuclear war simulation videos and made to show why nuclear war must never happen. Pick a scenario (NATO vs Russia, an attack on the United States, military targets only, escalation in Europe, United States vs China, or one bomb) and watch missiles arc from silo fields and submarines, warheads detonate, and an estimated death toll climb while the camera follows the action, with a clock, phases, a log and deaths by country. In Sandbox mode you decide who fires: pick a nuclear country, its missiles, how many warheads and how big, then click the map to launch or strike a whole country's cities or bases. Each country's warheads run out, and with "fire back" on, a country that's hit (or its nuclear ally) launches back, so you can watch one strike escalate. In Build your own mode, click anywhere to drop a warhead of any size (Hiroshima to Tsar Bomba) and see its fireball, blast, heat and fallout rings and an estimate of deaths and injuries. The numbers come from a simple model (blast and heat scaling rules of thumb and city populations over 50,000), so they're rough estimates
- **Typing Test** (`typing-test/`), **Tier List Maker** (`tier-list/`) and **Spin the Wheel** (`spin-wheel/`)

## Site-wide features
- **Quick jump:** press `Ctrl` + `K` (or `/`) on any page to search and jump to any app or game (`site.js`)
- **Install as an app + works offline:** the site can be installed from the browser (on an iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen), and pages and games keep working without internet (`manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`). Live data like news and weather still needs internet.
- **The app home screen** (`home.js`, `home.css`): the installed app opens on a phone-style home screen with a clock, a greeting, search, Jump back in, recently opened apps, every app and game as an icon, and a dock. Hold an icon (or tap ✏️) to drag apps around, hide them, pin them to the dock, or give them your own name, emoji and color. The 📱 button shows it in a browser too.
- **⚙️ Customize:** theme, accent color, background (aurora, glow, dots, plain or your own photo), font, text size, icon size and shape, grid or list, which parts to show, 12/24-hour clock, an app to open when the app starts, the 🏠 button that every other page gets inside the app (and its corner), calm mode (no animations anywhere), and backing up or restoring everything you've saved on the site.

The Browser includes Scramjet 1.1.0 (`browser/scramjet/`), Ultraviolet 3.2.10 (`browser/uv/`), bare-mux 2.1.9 (`browser/baremux/`, MIT License) and epoxy-transport 2.1.27 (`browser/epoxy/`), as published on npm. epoxy is loaded through `browser/tunnel.js`, which makes sure it only starts once even when many things are asked for at the same moment (starting it twice at once froze it). The only change to the packages is two lines at the top of `uv.bundle.js` and `scramjet.all.js` that let them work in browsers without shared workers (with `browser/baremux/shim.js`). `browser/scramjet/psl.json` is the [Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org/) (Mozilla Public License 2.0, taken from the [psl](https://www.npmjs.com/package/psl) package), handed to Scramjet so it never has to download it through a proxy server. Scramjet, Ultraviolet and epoxy come with GNU AGPL v3 license files; their license files are next to them, and their source code is at the links above.

Word Hunt checks guesses against the 5-letter words from [an-array-of-english-words](https://github.com/words/an-array-of-english-words) (MIT License).

Nuke Sim uses city populations from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) (CC BY 4.0, via the [all-the-cities](https://github.com/zeke/all-the-cities) package, MIT License), country shapes from Natural Earth (via world-atlas), Esri World Imagery map tiles, and public figures on arsenals and blast effects (the FAS Nuclear Notebook, Glasstone and Dolan's *The Effects of Nuclear Weapons*, and Wikipedia). Its missile silo dots are an illustrative random scatter, not real silo locations.

World Watch uses free public data: Wikipedia (Current events and the list of ongoing armed conflicts, CC BY-SA), the GDELT Project, USGS, NASA EONET, Where the ISS at?, RainViewer, OpenSky Network, Hacker News, Spaceflight News API, CoinGecko, Frankfurter, OpenStreetMap/Nominatim, Open-Meteo and Natural Earth (via world-atlas). Map tiles by CARTO, Esri and OpenStreetMap, drawn with [Leaflet](https://leafletjs.com/).
